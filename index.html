I've spent my time teaching students who didn't always believe they were good at what they were learning — and that's really the part I care about most. Not the syllabus, the moment someone stops saying "I can't" and starts asking "wait, what if."

Good teaching is mostly good listening.

Outside the classroom, I keep a journal. Nothing formal — just a place to put down what a day actually felt like, before I forget. Some of that finds its way here.

This page is deliberately simple, because that's closer to who I am: someone who'd rather have one honest conversation than perform for a room.

From the notebook

A few short entries — replace these with your own words whenever you're ready.

Entry one

On the quiet ones

The student who never raises a hand isn't always the one who doesn't know the answer. Sometimes they're just waiting to see if it's safe to be wrong out loud.

Entry two

A good question

I've stopped grading questions as "good" or "silly." A question is just someone standing at the edge of what they know, looking further.

Entry three

Why I still write by hand

Typing is faster, but slower thinking has its uses. The pen makes me finish a thought before starting the next one.
